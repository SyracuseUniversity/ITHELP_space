### **Overview**

To enhance email security and help users better identify potential threats, Syracuse University will enable Microsoft's native **External Email Tag** feature in Outlook.

The External Email Tag is an awareness tool designed to complement existing email security protections and help reduce the risk of phishing and social engineering attacks.

**What is an External Email Tag?**

When an email originates from outside Syracuse University, Outlook will display an **External** tag next to the sender's name. This feature provides a clear visual indicator that helps users recognize messages received from external senders and make more informed decisions when interacting with email content.

### **What counts as “External”?**

Microsoft 365 determines whether a message originated from inside or outside Syracuse University's email environment. Messages identified as originating from outside the University display the **External** tag in Outlook. This typically includes messages from vendors, partners, other colleges and universities, personal email accounts (such as Gmail, [Outlook.com](http://Outlook.com), or Yahoo), and other individuals or organizations outside Syracuse University.

A small number of trusted partner domains have been added to an exception list and may not display the tag. In addition, some University-approved services use third-party platforms to send email on behalf of Syracuse University, so messages generated by those systems may or may not display the External tag. The **External** tag indicates whether Microsoft 365 considers a message to have originated from outside the University's email environment; it does **not** indicate whether a message is safe, trusted, or malicious.

### **What to Expect**

- Email messages from external senders will display the **External** tag near the sender's name.
- Internal Syracuse University email messages will not display the tag.
- A limited number of trusted partner domains have been added to an exception list and will not display the tag.
- The banner is deployed centrally and cannot be disabled by individual users.
- The feature is provided by Microsoft and will appear consistently across:

  - Outlook for Windows (Classic and New Outlook)
  - Outlook for Mac
  - Outlook on the Web
  - Outlook for iOS
  - Outlook for Android

### **Why are we implementing this?**

Phishing attacks, business email compromise scams, and other social engineering attempts frequently originate from external email addresses. While many malicious messages are blocked before reaching users, no email security solution is perfect.

The External Email Tag serves as an additional visual cue that helps users:

- Recognize when an email originates from outside the University.
- Exercise caution before opening attachments or clicking links.
- Verify unexpected requests involving passwords, financial transactions, or sensitive information.
- Make more informed decisions when evaluating email messages.

### **Important Considerations**

The External Email Tag is a helpful indicator, but it should not be used as the sole factor in determining whether an email is legitimate.

#### **An External Tag Does Not Mean a Message Is Malicious**

Many legitimate emails originate from external organizations, including vendors, business partners, professional associations, conference organizers, and other institutions. These messages may display the External tag even though they are completely legitimate.

#### **No External Tag Does Not Mean a Message Is Safe**

The absence of an External tag simply indicates that the message originated from within Syracuse University's Microsoft 365 organization or from an approved exception domain.

If a Syracuse University account is compromised, an attacker may be able to send messages that appear internal and therefore do not display the External tag. For this reason, users should remain vigilant regardless of whether a message displays the tag.

### **What Should I Do?**

As always, be cautious when you receive any unexpected message, especially those that:

- Ask you to click a link.
- Request your password or personal information.
- Contain attachments you were not expecting.
- Create a sense of urgency.
- Request financial transactions, gift cards, or wire transfers.

When in doubt, verify the request through another communication method before taking action.

### **Reporting Suspicious Messages**

If you receive a suspicious email use Outlook's **Report Message** feature to flag it. For instructions see the [**Answers page on reporting phishing emails in Outlook**](https://maestro.syr.edu/trk/click?ref=zvu694kig_1-8c34x31ff84x0297&) .

Prompt reporting helps identify and address potential phishing campaigns affecting the University community.

### **Exceptions**

If you believe the communications your area is sending are internal and should not be marked as External Email, a request for an exception may be submitted by opening a ticket with **CISUNIX**. Each request will be reviewed to determine whether the domain meets the criteria for inclusion on the exception list.

If you need more information or assistance with verifying any email messages, please do not hesitate to contact your local IT support team.
